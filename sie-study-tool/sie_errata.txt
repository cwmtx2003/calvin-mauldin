# SIE Exam — Companion Errata & Updates

**Purpose.** This document is a companion to a 3rd-edition SIE prep manual. It identifies the topic areas most likely to be out of date in any prep book published before mid-2023 and provides the current rule, effective date, and a citation to a primary source (SEC, FINRA, MSRB, FinCEN, or the Federal Register). It does **not** reproduce or paraphrase the manual itself — use the two side by side.

**How to read each entry.**
- **Affected SIE topic** — the section of the FINRA SIE content outline where this appears.
- **What older prep books say** — the legacy state of the rule (described generically, not quoted).
- **Current rule** — what is true as of May 2026.
- **Effective date** — when the change took effect.
- **Citation** — primary-source link.
- **Exam-likelihood tag** — `HIGH` / `MEDIUM` / `LOW`, based on how central the topic is to the FINRA SIE content outline.

**Scope.** This pass focuses on high-impact areas: settlement cycle, conduct standards, capital-formation caps, money-market reform, short-sale reporting, senior-investor protection, margin, marketing rule, beneficial-ownership reporting, and the trading-holiday calendar. Items judged unchanged (basic securities products, the structure of FINRA/SEC/MSRB, customer-account types, AML pillars, etc.) are intentionally omitted.

## Section 1 — Knowledge of Capital Markets

### 1.1 Settlement cycle (T+2 → T+1)
- **Affected SIE topic.** Settlement of equity, corporate-bond, and municipal-bond trades; regular-way vs. cash settlement; ex-dividend dating.
- **What older prep books say.** Regular-way settlement for equities, corporate bonds, and municipals is **T+2**. Ex-dividend date is one business day before record date.
- **Current rule.** Regular-way settlement is **T+1**. Because settlement is now one business day after trade date, the ex-dividend date is the **same business day as the record date** for regular-way trades (FINRA aligned ex-date rules to T+1 in conjunction with the SEC change). Government securities and options remained at T+1 and T+0 respectively; cash settlement is still same-day; when-issued settles on issuance.
- **Effective date.** **May 28, 2024.**
- **Citation.** SEC, Final Rule: Shortening the Securities Transaction Settlement Cycle, Rel. No. 34-96930 (amending Rule 15c6-1); FINRA Technical Notice, *Final Reminder — T+1 Settlement* (May 22, 2024), <https://www.finra.org/filing-reporting/technical-notice/final-reminder-t-1-settlement-052224>.
- **Exam-likelihood: HIGH.** Settlement is tested on virtually every SIE form.

### 1.2 Juneteenth as a market holiday
- **Affected SIE topic.** Trading days, settlement-date arithmetic, ex-date arithmetic.
- **What older prep books say.** Lists nine U.S. market holidays (New Year's Day, MLK Day, Presidents' Day, Good Friday, Memorial Day, Independence Day, Labor Day, Thanksgiving, Christmas).
- **Current rule.** **Juneteenth (June 19)** is a federal holiday and an observed NYSE/Nasdaq/SIFMA bond-market closure. Use it when counting settlement days.
- **Effective date.** Federal-holiday status: June 17, 2021. First observed equity-market closure: 2022.
- **Citation.** SIFMA Holiday Schedule, <https://www.sifma.org/resources/guides-playbooks/holiday-schedule>; FINRA Operating & Holiday Schedule, <https://www.finra.org/about/operating-holiday-schedule>.
- **Exam-likelihood: MEDIUM.** Likely to surface inside a settlement-date counting question.

### 1.3 Regulation A — Tier 2 cap
- **Affected SIE topic.** Exempt offerings, "mini-IPO" framework.
- **What older prep books say.** Tier 2 of Reg A permits offerings up to **$50 million** in a 12-month period.
- **Current rule.** Tier 2 permits offerings up to **$75 million** in a 12-month period. Tier 1 remains at $20 million.
- **Effective date.** **March 15, 2021.**
- **Citation.** SEC, *Facilitating Capital Formation and Expanding Investment Opportunities by Improving Access to Capital in Private Markets*, Rel. No. 33-10884; SEC small-business resource page, <https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/exempt-offerings/regulation>.
- **Exam-likelihood: MEDIUM.**

### 1.4 Regulation Crowdfunding (Reg CF) — offering cap
- **Affected SIE topic.** Exempt offerings; crowdfunding intermediaries (funding portals).
- **What older prep books say.** The Reg CF cap is approximately **$1.07 million** (it was $1.07 M after the most recent pre-2021 inflation adjustment).
- **Current rule.** The Reg CF cap is **$5 million** per 12-month period. Investment limits for non-accredited investors are based on the **greater** of annual income or net worth (older books say the lesser). Accredited investors have no Reg CF investment limit.
- **Effective date.** **March 15, 2021** (same SEC rulemaking as the Reg A bump).
- **Citation.** SEC Reg CF resource page, <https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/exempt-offerings/regulation-crowdfunding>.
- **Exam-likelihood: MEDIUM.**

### 1.5 Rule 144 — current state
- **Affected SIE topic.** Resales of restricted and control securities.
- **What older prep books say.** Six-month holding for reporting issuers, one-year for non-reporting issuers; volume limits for affiliates.
- **Current rule.** The six-month / one-year holding-period framework is unchanged. The SEC's 2020 proposal to apply Rule 144 holding-period tolling to market-adjustable securities was adopted in part (Rel. No. 33-11070, Dec. 2022), and Form 144 must be filed **electronically on EDGAR** since the April 13, 2023 compliance date. If older material describes paper Form 144 filing, that is out of date.
- **Effective date.** EDGAR-only Form 144: **April 13, 2023.**
- **Citation.** SEC, *Electronic Submission of Applications for Orders under the Advisers Act and the Investment Company Act, Confidential Treatment Requests for Filings on Form 13F, and Form ACR*, Rel. No. 33-11070; SEC Rule 144 reference page, <https://www.sec.gov/reports/rule-144-selling-restricted-control-securities>.
- **Exam-likelihood: MEDIUM.**

## Section 2 — Understanding Products and Their Risks

### 2.1 Money-market fund reform (Rule 2a-7)
- **Affected SIE topic.** Money-market funds; institutional vs. retail/government MMFs.
- **What older prep books say.** Institutional prime and tax-exempt MMFs may impose **liquidity fees up to 2%** and **redemption gates up to 10 business days** when weekly liquid assets fall below 30%. Daily liquid assets ≥ 10%; weekly liquid assets ≥ 30%.
- **Current rule.** The SEC's 2023 reform **removed the redemption-gate authority** and replaced the legacy discretionary-fee framework with:
  - **Mandatory liquidity fee** for institutional prime and institutional tax-exempt funds when daily net redemptions **exceed 5%** of net assets (unless liquidity costs are less than one basis point).
  - **Discretionary liquidity fee** of up to 2% available to non-government MMFs when the board determines it is in the best interest of the fund.
  - **Higher minimum liquidity:** daily liquid assets raised to **25%** and weekly liquid assets raised to **50%**.
- **Effective date.** Rules effective Oct. 2, 2023. Mandatory-fee compliance date: **Oct. 2, 2024.** Form N-MFP/N-CR amendments effective June 11, 2024.
- **Citation.** SEC, *Money Market Fund Reforms*, Rel. No. IC-34959 (July 12, 2023), <https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2023/08/03/2023-15124/money-market-fund-reforms-form-pf-reporting-requirements-for-large-liquidity-fund-advisers-technical>.
- **Exam-likelihood: HIGH.** MMFs are a fixed SIE topic and the gate/fee language is exactly what older prep books drill.

### 2.2 Variable annuity disclosure form (Form N-4)
- **Affected SIE topic.** Variable annuities and the prospectus delivery requirement.
- **What older prep books say.** Variable annuity prospectus is a single, lengthy statutory document delivered before or with the sale.
- **Current rule.** Issuers of variable annuity and variable life contracts may satisfy prospectus delivery with a short-form **"Initial Summary Prospectus" (ISP)**, with the full statutory prospectus available online or on request — provided certain conditions are met.
- **Effective date.** Rule effective **July 1, 2020**; compliance with the updated tagged-data and amendment cycle requirements was phased through Jan. 1, 2022.
- **Citation.** SEC, *Updated Disclosure Requirements and Summary Prospectus for Variable Annuity and Variable Life Insurance Contracts*, Rel. No. 33-10765, <https://www.sec.gov/rules/final/2020/33-10765.pdf>.
- **Exam-likelihood: MEDIUM.**

### 2.3 Mutual fund "tailored shareholder reports" (Rule 30e-3 → Rule 30e-1/-3 redesign)
- **Affected SIE topic.** Mutual fund disclosure, shareholder reports.
- **What older prep books say.** Annual and semiannual reports are full statutory documents delivered in print or electronically under Rule 30e-3.
- **Current rule.** Open-end funds (mutual funds and ETFs) must deliver concise **Tailored Shareholder Reports (TSRs)** to retail shareholders; lengthier financial data moves to Form N-CSR and the fund website. Rule 30e-3 was rescinded for open-end funds in the same release.
- **Effective date.** Rules effective Jan. 24, 2023; **compliance date July 24, 2024.**
- **Citation.** SEC, *Tailored Shareholder Reports for Mutual Funds and Exchange-Traded Funds*, Rel. No. 33-11125, <https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/final/2022/33-11125.pdf>.
- **Exam-likelihood: LOW-MEDIUM.** Possible disclosure question.

### 2.4 Digital assets / crypto in the brokerage context
- **Affected SIE topic.** Securities products, alternative investments, custody.
- **What older prep books say.** Treats crypto as outside FINRA jurisdiction or barely addresses it.
- **Current rule (status, not a single bright-line rule).**
  - **Spot Bitcoin ETPs** were approved by the SEC on Jan. 10, 2024; **spot Ether ETPs** approved May 23, 2024. These trade as registered '40 Act/exchange-traded products and are within scope of the FINRA-registered representative's universe.
  - **SAB 121 was rescinded** by SAB 122 on Jan. 23, 2025, removing the on-balance-sheet treatment requirement for custodied crypto.
  - Broker-dealers handling crypto-asset securities remain subject to FINRA Rule 3110 supervision and Reg BI when making recommendations involving these products.
- **Citation.** SEC, *Order Granting Accelerated Approval of Proposed Rule Changes to List and Trade Bitcoin-Based Commodity-Based Trust Shares*, Rel. No. 34-99306 (Jan. 10, 2024), <https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/nysearca/2024/34-99306.pdf>; SEC Staff Accounting Bulletin 122, <https://www.sec.gov/oca/staff-accounting-bulletin-122>.
- **Exam-likelihood: LOW-MEDIUM.** Unlikely to dominate the exam but high-conceptual-relevance.

## Section 3 — Understanding Trading, Customer Accounts and Prohibited Activities

### 3.1 Regulation Best Interest (Reg BI) and Form CRS
- **Affected SIE topic.** Standards of conduct for broker-dealers; suitability vs. best-interest.
- **What older prep books say.** Suitability (FINRA Rule 2111) is the governing standard for broker-dealer recommendations to retail customers.
- **Current rule.** **Reg BI (Exchange Act Rule 15*l*-1)** is the governing standard for broker-dealer recommendations to retail customers. It imposes four obligations: **Disclosure, Care, Conflict of Interest, and Compliance.** Older "suitability" language is now subsumed within the Care Obligation. **Form CRS** (Form ADV Part 3 for advisers, Form CRS for BDs) must be delivered to each retail customer at or before the recommendation/account opening.
- **Effective date.** **Compliance date June 30, 2020.**
- **Citation.** SEC, *Regulation Best Interest: The Broker-Dealer Standard of Conduct*, Rel. No. 34-86031, <https://www.sec.gov/rules/final/2019/34-86031.pdf>; FINRA Reg BI key-topic page, <https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/key-topics/regulation-best-interest>.
- **Exam-likelihood: HIGH.** Reg BI is a tested SIE topic and prep books published before mid-2020 are wrong here.

### 3.2 FINRA Rule 2165 — financial exploitation of specified adults
- **Affected SIE topic.** Protection of senior investors and "specified adults"; temporary holds.
- **What older prep books say.** A member firm may place a temporary hold on a **disbursement** of funds or securities for up to **15 business days**, extendable by 10 business days, when there is a reasonable belief of financial exploitation of a specified adult (age 65+, or 18+ with mental/physical impairment).
- **Current rule.** A firm may now also place a temporary hold on a **securities transaction** (not just on a disbursement). The maximum hold may be extended an additional **30 business days** beyond the 25-business-day cap if the matter has been reported to a state regulator, agency, or court of competent jurisdiction — for a total of **up to 55 business days.**
- **Effective date.** **March 17, 2022.**
- **Citation.** FINRA, *Regulatory Notice 22-05* (Feb. 17, 2022), <https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/22-05>; SEC Order Approving SR-FINRA-2021-016 (Jan. 25, 2022), <https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/finra/2022/34-94061.pdf>.
- **Exam-likelihood: HIGH.** The 15/10/55-business-day arithmetic is a classic SIE trap.

### 3.3 FINRA Rule 3241 — registered person as customer's beneficiary or in a position of trust
- **Affected SIE topic.** Conflicts of interest, prohibited practices, senior-investor protection.
- **What older prep books say.** Not present (rule did not exist in the 1st/2nd edition vintage). Often handled implicitly under firm policy.
- **Current rule.** A registered person who is **named as a customer's beneficiary, executor, trustee, or attorney-in-fact, or who receives a bequest from a customer's estate**, must provide written notice to the firm and obtain firm review/approval before assuming that status. The rule does **not** apply where the customer is a member of the registered person's **immediate family** (as defined).
- **Effective date.** **February 15, 2021.**
- **Citation.** FINRA, *Regulatory Notice 20-38*, <https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/20-38>.
- **Exam-likelihood: MEDIUM.** Often tested as a "trustee/POA" prohibited-practice scenario.

### 3.4 FINRA Rule 4210 — covered agency transactions margin
- **Affected SIE topic.** Margin requirements, mortgage-backed securities, TBA market.
- **What older prep books say.** A 2% maintenance-margin requirement applies to non-exempt accounts for Covered Agency Transactions (TBA MBS, specified pools, certain agency CMOs).
- **Current rule.** The **2% maintenance-margin requirement was eliminated.** Members must instead collect **variation margin** on mark-to-market losses on Covered Agency Transactions, subject to a **$250,000 per-counterparty threshold.** Members may, subject to conditions, take a capital charge in lieu of collecting variation margin.
- **Effective date.** **May 22, 2024.**
- **Citation.** FINRA, *Frequently Asked Questions Regarding Covered Agency Transaction Margin*, <https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/key-topics/margin-accounts/4210faq>; SEC Order Approving SR-FINRA-2021-010 (July 27, 2023).
- **Exam-likelihood: LOW-MEDIUM.** The SIE rarely drills the TBA mechanics, but the 2% number is wrong if cited.

### 3.5 Short-sale reporting — Rule 13f-2 / Form SHO (status note)
- **Affected SIE topic.** Reg SHO, short selling, public reporting of short positions.
- **What older prep books say.** Short-position public reporting is limited to FINRA's twice-monthly aggregate-short-interest data.
- **Current rule (with a caveat).** SEC adopted **Rule 13f-2** and **Form SHO**, requiring institutional investment managers above specified thresholds to file monthly short-position reports; the SEC will publish aggregated data publicly. Rule was effective Jan. 2, 2024 with a Jan. 2, 2025 compliance date — but the **SEC has extended the compliance/first-filing date several times; as of Dec. 2025 the first Form SHO filings are not due until Feb. 14, 2028,** pending the SEC's response to the Fifth Circuit's remand. The underlying rule remains on the books.
- **Citation.** SEC, *Short Position and Short Activity Reporting by Institutional Investment Managers*, Rel. No. 34-98738 (Oct. 13, 2023); SEC Press Release 2025-37, <https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2025-37>; Morgan Lewis, *Short Sale Reporting on Form SHO: Compliance Date Further Extended to 2028* (Dec. 2025), <https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/12/short-sale-reporting-on-form-sho-compliance-date-further-extended-to-2028>.
- **Exam-likelihood: LOW.** Tested at most as a "name the form" item; the extended compliance date means SIE writers are unlikely to lean on it.

### 3.6 SEC Marketing Rule (Rule 206(4)-1) — note for adviser-side questions
- **Affected SIE topic.** Investment adviser advertising. (Note: the SIE is primarily a BD-side exam, but the Investment Advisers Act framework is in scope.)
- **What older prep books say.** Two separate rules govern adviser advertising and cash solicitation: the old Rule 206(4)-1 (advertising) and Rule 206(4)-3 (cash solicitation). Testimonials are effectively prohibited.
- **Current rule.** The two old rules were **combined into a single Marketing Rule** (Rule 206(4)-1). Testimonials and endorsements are **permitted subject to disclosure, oversight, and disqualification conditions.** Performance presentations are subject to net-of-fees and time-period requirements.
- **Effective date.** Rule effective May 4, 2021. **Compliance date November 4, 2022.**
- **Citation.** SEC, *Investment Adviser Marketing*, Rel. No. IA-5653, <https://www.sec.gov/rules/final/2020/ia-5653.pdf>.
- **Exam-likelihood: LOW-MEDIUM.**

### 3.7 Beneficial-ownership reporting (Corporate Transparency Act) — status note for AML/KYC
- **Affected SIE topic.** Anti-money-laundering, customer-identification program (CIP), beneficial-ownership identification.
- **What older prep books say.** The 2018 FinCEN CDD Rule requires covered financial institutions to identify and verify the beneficial owners of legal-entity customers (25% equity / one control person).
- **Current rule.**
  - The **FinCEN CDD Rule for covered financial institutions remains in force unchanged** — broker-dealers must still identify and verify beneficial owners of legal-entity customers at account opening.
  - Separately, the **Corporate Transparency Act (CTA)** required most U.S. entities to file Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI) reports directly with FinCEN. **As of the March 26, 2025 interim final rule, FinCEN has removed BOI reporting requirements for U.S. companies and U.S. persons;** only foreign reporting companies registered to do business in the U.S. remain subject. Multiple court challenges are pending. If your prep book treats CTA BOI as a universal U.S. requirement, that is now wrong.
- **Effective dates.** FinCEN CDD Rule: May 11, 2018 (unchanged). CTA BOI scope reduction: **March 26, 2025** interim final rule.
- **Citation.** FinCEN, *Beneficial Ownership Information Reporting*, <https://www.fincen.gov/boi>; FinCEN press release, *FinCEN Removes Beneficial Ownership Reporting Requirements for U.S. Companies and U.S. Persons* (Mar. 21, 2025), <https://www.fincen.gov/news/news-releases/fincen-removes-beneficial-ownership-reporting-requirements-us-companies-and-us>.
- **Exam-likelihood: MEDIUM.** AML/BSA is a fixed SIE topic; the CDD Rule (not the CTA) is what the SIE actually tests, so the practical answer for exam purposes is: stick with the CDD Rule, ignore the CTA noise.

## Section 4 — Overview of the Regulatory Framework

### 4.1 Consolidated Audit Trail (CAT) — operational
- **Affected SIE topic.** Order-audit-trail systems (older books still reference OATS).
- **What older prep books say.** Broker-dealers report equity order activity through **OATS** (Order Audit Trail System).
- **Current rule.** **OATS was retired on September 1, 2021.** All in-scope order-event reporting moved to the **Consolidated Audit Trail (CAT).** FINRA Rule 6800 Series governs member compliance.
- **Effective date.** OATS retirement: **Sept. 1, 2021.** CAT reporting has been operational and phased in since 2020.
- **Citation.** FINRA, *2024 Annual Regulatory Oversight Report — CAT*, <https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/guidance/reports/2024-finra-annual-regulatory-oversight-report/cat>; CAT NMS Plan, <https://www.catnmsplan.com/>.
- **Exam-likelihood: MEDIUM.** "OATS" is a frequent wrong-answer choice on the current SIE; "CAT" is correct.

### 4.2 SIPC coverage limits (unchanged — included as a guard against misremembered numbers)
- **Affected SIE topic.** Securities Investor Protection Corporation.
- **Current rule.** SIPC protects each customer up to **$500,000 in securities and cash, of which up to $250,000 may be cash.** Numbers have not changed since 2010; if a prep book lists different numbers, it is wrong.
- **Citation.** SIPC, *What SIPC Protects*, <https://www.sipc.org/for-investors/what-sipc-protects>.
- **Exam-likelihood: HIGH (always tested).**

### 4.3 MSRB — Rule G-15 / G-12 alignment with T+1
- **Affected SIE topic.** Municipal-bond settlement, MSRB rulebook.
- **What older prep books say.** MSRB Rules G-12 (inter-dealer) and G-15 (customer) set regular-way municipal settlement at T+2.
- **Current rule.** MSRB amended Rules G-12 and G-15 to align with the SEC's T+1 transition. Regular-way municipal settlement is **T+1.**
- **Effective date.** **May 28, 2024.**
- **Citation.** MSRB, *MSRB Files Amendments to Move Municipal Securities to T+1 Settlement Cycle*, <https://www.msrb.org/sites/default/files/2023-12/2023-19.pdf>.
- **Exam-likelihood: HIGH.** Settlement is core SIE content.

### 4.4 FINRA Rule 3110.19 — Residential Supervisory Location ("hybrid work" rule)
- **Affected SIE topic.** Supervision and OSJ/branch-office classification.
- **What older prep books say.** A registered representative's home location used for securities business is generally treated as a branch office or non-branch location subject to specified supervisory inspections.
- **Current rule.** FINRA Rule 3110.19 created a new category — **Residential Supervisory Location (RSL)** — permitting a registered person's private residence to be classified as a non-branch location subject to a **periodic (rather than annual) inspection cycle**, provided enumerated conditions are met (no customer funds at the location, no public-facing signage, eligible-person status, etc.).
- **Effective date.** **June 1, 2024.**
- **Citation.** FINRA, *Regulatory Notice 24-02*, <https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/24-02>.
- **Exam-likelihood: LOW-MEDIUM.**

### 4.5 FINRA Rule 3110.18 — Remote Inspections Pilot Program
- **Affected SIE topic.** Supervision and branch-office inspection.
- **What older prep books say.** Branch-office inspections must be conducted **on-site** at the prescribed cadence.
- **Current rule.** FINRA's three-year **Remote Inspections Pilot Program** permits eligible firms to satisfy Rule 3110(c) inspection obligations remotely, subject to enrollment, written supervisory procedures, and data reporting to FINRA.
- **Effective date.** **July 1, 2024**, through June 30, 2027.
- **Citation.** FINRA, *Regulatory Notice 24-02*, <https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/notices/24-02>.
- **Exam-likelihood: LOW.**

## What was removed (and why)

The following older content categories typically appear in pre-2023 SIE prep manuals and should be **deleted or annotated as stale** when studying:

1. **"T+2 regular-way settlement."** Replaced by T+1 (see §1.1). Any practice question keyed on T+2 ex-date arithmetic is now wrong.
2. **"Suitability is the broker-dealer's standard for retail recommendations."** Reg BI superseded this (see §3.1). Suitability survives only inside Reg BI's Care Obligation and as a standalone rule for institutional accounts.
3. **"Reg A Tier 2 is capped at $50 million" / "Reg CF is capped at ~$1.07 million."** Both caps were raised (§§1.3, 1.4).
4. **"Form 144 may be filed in paper."** EDGAR-only since April 13, 2023 (§1.5).
5. **"Money-market funds may impose redemption gates."** Gates were removed in the 2023 reform; the institutional-prime/tax-exempt liquidity-fee framework is now mandatory above the 5% net-redemption trigger (§2.1).
6. **"OATS reporting."** OATS was retired Sept. 1, 2021 — replaced by CAT (§4.1).
7. **"2% maintenance margin on Covered Agency Transactions."** Replaced by variation-margin requirement (§3.4).
8. **"FINRA Rule 2165 hold is up to 25 business days and applies only to disbursements."** It now extends to transactions, and the cap can reach 55 business days with regulator notification (§3.2).
9. **"Juneteenth is not a market holiday."** It is (§1.2).
10. **"Investment-adviser testimonials are prohibited."** Permitted under conditions since Marketing Rule compliance date Nov. 4, 2022 (§3.6).
11. **"MSRB Rules G-12 and G-15 require T+2 settlement for municipals."** Now T+1 (§4.3).
12. **"A registered rep's home location is a branch unless explicitly excepted."** RSL classification now exists (§4.4).

## What was *not* changed (intentional non-edits)

The following are commonly suspected of being out of date but **are not** as of May 2026, and any prep-book treatment of them is still correct:

- The four pillars of an AML program plus the independent-test requirement (FINRA Rule 3310).
- SIPC limits of $500,000 / $250,000-cash per separate-capacity customer.
- The "two-out-of-three" test for U.S. tax residency / treatment of foreign investors is **not** an SIE topic — ignore conflicting prep-book content.
- The Securities Act of 1933 / Exchange Act of 1934 / Investment Company Act of 1940 / Investment Advisers Act of 1940 / Trust Indenture Act of 1939 / Securities Investor Protection Act of 1970 framework.
- FINRA registration exams (SIE + top-off Series 6 / 7 / 79 / 22, etc.) — structure unchanged.
- Customer-identification program (CIP) elements: name, DOB, address, taxpayer ID number.
- Standard order types (market, limit, stop, stop-limit), good-til-cancel duration.

## How to use this document during your prep

1. Read the manual chapter normally.
2. Cross-reference any section listed above and **annotate the manual margin with the corrected rule + effective date** (don't try to memorize two versions).
3. Trust this document — not the manual — on every item in the "What was removed" list.
4. For the items tagged **HIGH** in exam-likelihood (T+1, Reg BI, Rule 2165, MMF reform, MSRB T+1, SIPC limits), expect multiple questions on test day. Drill these to recognition speed.
5. If a practice-question bank gives you an answer that conflicts with this document, the question is stale — not the rule.

# APPENDIX A — Unit-by-Unit Audit of the Kaplan SIE LEM, 3rd Ed. (©2022, updated July 2023)

This appendix walks the manual unit by unit. For each unit I list the stated **Learning Objectives** (as printed at the start of the unit), then flag every claim that is **STALE**, **OK** (current as printed), or **ADD** (a current rule the manual does not cover but the SIE may test). Page numbers refer to the printed page numbers in the Kaplan PDF.

**Headline finding.** The 3rd Edition (updated July 2023) is **more current than a typical pre-2023 prep book** — it already reflects Reg BI, Form CRS, Reg A+ $75M, the Coverdell/529 framework, ADRs, and the post-2020 conduct rules. The principal gaps are:

1. **Settlement is T+2 throughout the manual** (Units 1, 2, 3). The May 2024 T+1 transition is not reflected anywhere. This is the single biggest correction.
2. **FINRA Rule 2165** (Unit 11) is described in pre-2022-amendment form — 15-business-day hold on disbursements only. The 2022 amendments (transaction holds; up to 55-business-day extension) are missing.
3. **FINRA Rule 3241** (registered person as beneficiary/POA/trustee) is missing entirely from Unit 12.
4. **2023 Money Market Fund reforms** (Unit 4) are not reflected — the manual still describes the legacy redemption-gate / discretionary-liquidity-fee framework where MMFs are discussed.
5. **SECURE 2.0 retirement changes** (Unit 6) are partially or not reflected — RMD age moved to 73, the 50% RMD-missed penalty was reduced, and the IRA catch-up is now indexed.
6. **FINRA Rule 3110.18 / 3110.19** (Unit 12) — Remote Inspections Pilot and Residential Supervisory Location are not mentioned (both took effect after the July 2023 update).
7. **SEC Regulation S-P 2024 amendments** (Unit 11) — incident-response and 30-day customer notification requirements are not in the book.

What follows is the unit-by-unit detail.

## Global
- Option-exercise settlement: when an equity (stock) option is EXERCISED, the resulting stock delivery now settles T+1 (aligned with regular-way equity settlement, effective 5/28/2024) — NOT T+2. Index options settle the next business day in cash (intrinsic value). Any statement that an equity option exercise settles "T+2 after exercise" is STALE; use T+1.
